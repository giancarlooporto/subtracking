## 2. Competitive Context & Psychological Trigger

Bobby (developed by Yummygum) established the gold standard for manual subscription tracking on iOS in the late 2010s. Its vibrant color-coded cards, tactile interface, and strict refusal to mandate bank credentials won a devoted following among privacy-conscious consumers who rejected invasive aggregators like Mint and Truebill.

However, Bobby has suffered from **severe platform and feature stagnation**:
1. **Apple Ecosystem Exclusivity**: Bobby has never released an official Android app, Windows desktop client, or full web interface. Users switching to Android or managing finances on a desktop workstation are completely locked out.
2. **Development Stagnation**: Major feature development and analytical enhancements have largely ceased. Bobby remains a static list of cards with basic monthly/yearly sum totals.
3. **Fragile Sync & Backup**: Users report recurring iCloud synchronization failures across iPads and iPhones, risking data loss when upgrading devices.
4. **No Deep Financial Intelligence**: Bobby shows what subscriptions cost today, but provides zero insight into long-term compounding burn ("Ghost Costs") and offers no streamlined audit mechanism to purge forgotten services.

SubTracking solves these exact pain points. It takes everything users love about Bobby—sleek visual cards, zero bank logins, pure privacy—and expands it into a **universal cross-platform Progressive Web App (PWA)** with **10-year Ghost Cost compounding calculations**, an interactive **Tinder-style Audit Wizard**, and **optional $8.99/year end-to-end encrypted cloud sync**.

### H2: Where Bobby Excels (And Where It Leaves You Stranded)

#### H3: The Pioneer of Manual Subscription Tracking
Bobby proved that consumers do not want third-party bots constantly scraping their bank accounts. Its clean, card-based interface made tracking Netflix, Spotify, and iCloud intuitive and private. For single-device iPhone users with static subscription lists, Bobby was a breath of fresh air.

#### H3: The Wall Around Apple: Why Android and Desktop Users Are Forgotten
Modern digital life is multi-device. Millions of users carry an Android phone and use a MacBook, or carry an iPhone and work on a Windows workstation. Bobby's strict refusal to build a browser-accessible web application or an Android APK forces users into artificial silos. The moment you leave the Apple ecosystem, your subscription data is trapped.

#### H3: Beyond Basic Sums: The Need for Opportunity Cost Modeling
Knowing that your subscriptions sum to $120/month is useful, but it does not deter cognitive spending creep. Bobby fails to show what that $120/month actually represents over a 5-year or 10-year horizon. SubTracking bridges this psychological gap by demonstrating how small recurring leaks compound into major wealth destruction.
