## 2. Competitive Context & Psychological Trigger

DIY financial spreadsheets (Excel, Google Sheets, Notion) are the default weapon of choice for tech-savvy, privacy-conscious consumers who refuse to connect their bank accounts to automated aggregators like Mint or Rocket Money. Spreadsheets provide complete privacy, 100% user control, and zero monthly subscription costs.

However, studies reveal that **over 70% of DIY budget spreadsheets are abandoned within 90 days** due to **"Spreadsheet Fatigue"**:
1. **Broken Formulas & Cell Corruption**: Accidental cell edits, deleted rows, and broken `=SUM()` or `=DATE()` formulas turn maintenance into debugging chores.
2. **Zero Automated Renewal Alerts**: Spreadsheets cannot push a native calendar alert to your phone 3 days before a $180 annual subscription or free trial renews. Users still get caught by surprise auto-renewals.
3. **Severe Mobile Input Friction**: Updating a complex spreadsheet on a 6-inch smartphone screen requires constant pinching, zooming, and typing into tiny formula bars.
4. **Static Sums vs. Opportunity Cost**: Spreadsheets tell you what you spend each month, but rarely calculate the multi-year compound opportunity cost of recurring micro-commitments.

SubTracking bridges this gap: it delivers **all the privacy, offline control, and zero-bank security of a private spreadsheet**, combined with the **fluid UX, automated calendar alerts, 10-year Ghost Cost math, and 60-second Audit Wizard** of a dedicated web utility—completely free locally, with an optional $8.99/year encrypted cloud pass.

### H2: The Hidden Friction of DIY Budget Sheets: Why 70% of Spreadsheets Fail

#### H3: 1. "Spreadsheet Decay" and the Maintenance Tax
Building a custom Google Sheet or Excel workbook feels empowering on day one. You format color-coded cells, build dropdown menus, and configure sum totals. But by month two or three, updating the sheet becomes a chore. One accidental formula overwrite breaks your annual total; adding a bi-weekly subscription requires adjusting cell ranges; and categorizing streaming trials slips down your weekend to-do list.

#### H3: 2. The Silent Killer: Missing Renewal Deadlines
The primary purpose of tracking subscriptions is catching charges *before* they occur. A spreadsheet is entirely passive: it sits closed on your computer drive, incapable of notifying you when a 7-day free trial is about to expire or when an annual Adobe or Amazon Prime contract is scheduled to auto-renew. By the time you open your spreadsheet to log the expense, the money has already left your bank account.

#### H3: 3. The Smartphone Usability Nightmare
When you sign up for a new software trial or mobile app on your phone, you want to log it in 10 seconds. Attempting to open Google Sheets or Microsoft Excel on a mobile screen requires waiting for heavy mobile apps to load, zooming in on miniature grid cells, and battling predictive keyboard input. Most users procrastinate, forget, and allow subscription creep to resume.
